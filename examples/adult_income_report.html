What was decided

  • Target: class (classification, scored by roc_auc)
  • One row: one row = one adult
  • Split: Trained on 39,032 rows, tested on 9,758 held-out rows, split keeping the same mix of outcomes in both.
  • Duplicates: exact copy rows were dropped before anything else.

What the data showed

Most informative on their own: relationship, marital_status, capital_gain, age, education_num, education.

  • capital_gain has a long tail (skew 12.1; 0.0% of values are extreme). A few huge values pull linear models around; trees do not mind. → Added capital_gain__log, the log of it, which spreads the values out.
  • capital_loss has a long tail (skew 4.6; 0.0% of values are extreme). A few huge values pull linear models around; trees do not mind. → Added capital_loss__log, the log of it, which spreads the values out.
  • hours_per_week has extreme values on 13.6% of rows. Worth checking they are real, not typos.
  • workclass is missing on 6% of rows, and that is itself a clue: the outcome rate is 9% when it is missing against 25% when not. → Kept as a signal: training adds a 'was missing' flag next to the filled-in value.
  • occupation is missing on 6% of rows, and that is itself a clue: the outcome rate is 9% when it is missing against 25% when not. → Kept as a signal: training adds a 'was missing' flag next to the filled-in value.
  • 4 training rows (under 0.1%) repeat another row's features exactly. If they are copies, a copy in the test rows makes the score look better than it is. → Check the row key in the contract; repeated rows usually mean a join or an export went wrong.
